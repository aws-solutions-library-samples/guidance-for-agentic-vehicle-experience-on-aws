# P0420 — Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1)

## Summary
The PCM has detected that the catalytic converter on Bank 1 is not operating at expected efficiency. The downstream O2 sensor readings mirror the upstream sensor, indicating the catalyst is no longer reducing emissions effectively.

## Severity: P2 — Service Soon
Not a safety concern. Vehicle is drivable. Schedule service within 1-2 weeks to avoid emissions test failure.

## Common Causes (by likelihood)
1. **Aged catalytic converter** (60%) — Normal degradation after 80,000-120,000 miles
2. **Faulty downstream O2 sensor** (20%) — Sensor reading incorrectly
3. **Exhaust leak before catalyst** (10%) — Introduces unmetered air
4. **Engine misfire** (5%) — Unburned fuel damages catalyst over time
5. **Incorrect fuel type** (5%) — Leaded fuel or contaminated gas

## Diagnostic Steps
1. Verify no other DTCs present (especially P0300-P0308 misfires)
2. Check downstream O2 sensor waveform — should be flat/steady vs upstream oscillation
3. Inspect exhaust for leaks between manifold and catalyst
4. Check catalyst inlet/outlet temperature differential (should be 50-100°F higher at outlet)
5. If sensor and exhaust are good, catalyst replacement is indicated

## Driver Communication
> "Your vehicle's catalytic converter isn't cleaning exhaust gases as efficiently as it should. This won't affect how the car drives, but you'll want to get it checked within the next couple of weeks — especially if you have an emissions inspection coming up."

## Repair Estimate
- O2 sensor replacement: $150-$300
- Catalytic converter replacement: $1,000-$2,500 (parts + labor)
- Exhaust leak repair: $100-$400
