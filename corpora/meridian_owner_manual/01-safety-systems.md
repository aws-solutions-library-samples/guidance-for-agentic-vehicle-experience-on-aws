# Safety Systems — Automatic and Active Features

Meridian vehicles are equipped with a suite of automatic and active safety systems that work together to reduce collision risk and support driver awareness. This page describes the main categories of safety technology present across the Meridian lineup and explains how each system behaves during normal driving.

## Forward Collision Warning and Automatic Emergency Braking

Forward collision warning monitors the road ahead using a front-facing sensor array and alerts the driver with a visual and audible signal when a potential collision is detected. If the driver does not respond and the system determines that a collision is imminent, automatic emergency braking applies the brakes without driver input to reduce impact severity or avoid the collision entirely.

Automatic emergency braking operates at a range of speeds and is calibrated to detect both vehicles and pedestrians. The system does not replace attentive driving — it is designed as a secondary safety measure for situations in which the driver is unable to react in time. The driver can deactivate forward collision warning alerts through the vehicle settings menu; automatic emergency braking cannot be permanently disabled and will remain active.

## Lane Departure Warning and Lane Keeping Assist

Lane departure warning monitors lane markings using front cameras and alerts the driver with a haptic steering wheel vibration when the vehicle begins to drift out of its lane without an active turn signal. Lane keeping assist goes one step further by applying gentle steering correction to guide the vehicle back toward the lane center.

Both systems require visible lane markings to function reliably. In conditions of heavy rain, snow, or faded road markings, system performance may be reduced and a dashboard indicator will notify the driver. Lane keeping assist can be toggled off from the driver assistance settings panel.

## Blind Spot Monitoring

Blind spot monitoring uses rear-quarter sensors to detect vehicles travelling in adjacent lanes beside the Meridian. When a vehicle enters the monitored zone, an indicator light illuminates in the corresponding door mirror. If the driver activates the turn signal toward an occupied adjacent lane, the indicator pulses and an audible alert sounds.

## Rear Cross-Traffic Alert

When reversing out of a parking space, rear cross-traffic alert uses rear sensors to detect vehicles, cyclists, or pedestrians approaching from the side. An audible tone and a dashboard warning notify the driver before the obstruction enters the vehicle's reverse path. The system supplements the reversing camera display rather than replacing it.

## Adaptive Cruise Control

Adaptive cruise control maintains a set following distance behind a detected vehicle ahead, automatically adjusting speed up to the driver-set cruise speed. When traffic stops, the system brings the Meridian to a standstill and resumes travel when the driver taps the accelerator or pressing the resume button, depending on how long the vehicle has been stationary.

Adaptive cruise control is suitable for motorway and highway driving. It is not designed for urban intersections or stop-and-go traffic at low speeds without active driver supervision.

## Driver Attention Monitoring

A driver-facing camera and onboard analysis monitor steering input patterns and driving behaviour. When the system detects sustained inattention — such as prolonged absence of steering correction — it displays a coffee-cup icon on the instrument cluster and suggests taking a break. Prolonged alerts will escalate to an audible notification. The system cannot intervene in vehicle control based on driver attention status alone; its role is notification only.

## Over-the-Air Safety Configuration Updates

Safety system calibration parameters and software can be updated over-the-air during scheduled update windows. Updates are confirmed on the infotainment display before being applied. No safety system is modified mid-journey; updates are staged and installed only when the vehicle is stationary and connected to a known network.

## When Safety Systems Are Not a Substitute for Attention

All of the systems described in this section are designed to assist an attentive driver — they are not substitutes for maintaining full attention on the road, keeping both hands on the wheel, and following all applicable road rules. Conditions such as bright sunlight, adverse weather, obscured sensors, or unusual road environments can affect system performance. The driver is always responsible for safe vehicle operation.
