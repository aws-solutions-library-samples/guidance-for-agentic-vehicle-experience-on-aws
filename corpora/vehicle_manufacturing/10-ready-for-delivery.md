# Ready for Delivery — Final Steps Before Customer Handover

The ready-for-delivery status is the final state in the vehicle's manufacturing and distribution pipeline. It indicates that the dealer's pre-delivery inspection is complete, all accessories have been fitted, all documentation is prepared, and the vehicle is waiting only for the customer to arrive and take the keys. This stage spans the period from PDI completion to the moment the customer signs the delivery documentation and drives away — commonly one to five business days.

## What Happens Before a Vehicle Is Ready for Delivery

The transition from "at dealer" to "ready for delivery" requires several steps to be confirmed complete:

**PDI sign-off.** The dealer's technician marks the pre-delivery inspection as complete in the manufacturer's dealer management system (DMS). Any defects found during PDI and subsequently rectified are documented with their resolutions. If any defect requires parts that are not in stock, the vehicle does not advance to ready-for-delivery status until the parts are received, fitted, and re-inspected.

**Registration.** In most markets, the vehicle must be registered with the national vehicle authority before it can be handed to the customer. The dealer handles registration on behalf of the customer using the information provided at order. Registration assigns the vehicle's number plate and generates the registration certificate. For many customers, this is the step they associate most closely with "it's nearly mine."

**Finance and insurance confirmation.** If the customer is financing or leasing the vehicle, the finance company must confirm that all documentation has been executed correctly and that funds are ready to settle to the dealer. The vehicle cannot change ownership without this confirmation. Similarly, the customer's insurance policy must be confirmed as active from the handover date.

**Key and documentation preparation.** The dealer prepares the documentation pack that will be handed to the customer: the owner's handbook (or, increasingly, a digital reference to the online handbook), the service book or digital service record, the warranty documentation, the vehicle registration certificate, the PDI record, and any accessory fitting records. Both sets of keys are verified as present and functioning.

**Connected vehicle provisioning.** For vehicles with a connected vehicle service platform, the delivery step activates the customer's account link to the vehicle's VIN. The customer's connected app is provisioned so that, from the moment of key handover, the customer can view the vehicle's charge state, lock and unlock remotely, set departure charges or pre-conditioning, and access journey data. In programmes that use digital keys, the primary digital key is provisioned during this step.

**Vehicle presentation.** The dealer prepares the vehicle physically for presentation: a final clean (exterior wash, interior vacuum, glass clean), tyre pressure verification at delivery specification, and confirmation that the vehicle is positioned in the handover bay or showroom at the correct state of charge if electric. A vehicle that arrives at handover looking dirty or unkempt, or with a low state of charge, creates a poor first impression that undermines the entire delivery journey regardless of build quality.

## The Customer Handover Appointment

The handover appointment is the event through which the ready-for-delivery vehicle is formally transferred to the customer. A structured handover covers:

**Documentation signing.** The customer signs the delivery acceptance paperwork, confirming the vehicle's condition at handover and acknowledging receipt of the documentation pack.

**Vehicle walk-around.** The delivery specialist walks the customer around the vehicle, pointing out the key exterior features, confirming the condition is as expected, and noting any pre-agreed minor imperfections.

**Feature induction.** Depending on the customer's preference, the delivery specialist demonstrates the key controls: powertrain mode selection, infotainment and navigation, charging setup, safety system activation and operation, and any features specific to the model or package. For customers who have been on a pre-delivery ownership programme, this step is often abbreviated; for new-to-brand customers, it may run thirty minutes or more.

**Connected app pairing.** The customer is walked through linking their connected vehicle app to the vehicle, confirming that location, status, and remote control are functioning before they leave the forecourt.

**First charge guidance (electric vehicles).** For electric vehicle customers, the delivery specialist confirms the customer's home charging setup is in place, reviews the charge scheduling settings, and ensures the customer understands the difference between regular daily charging and occasional DC rapid charging.

## Why the Ready-for-Delivery Stage Can Take Longer Than Expected

The most common reasons a vehicle that has reached the dealer is not immediately ready for delivery are: registration processing delays (public authority backlogs in some markets), finance company documentation hold-ups, parts awaited for accessory fitment, or simply PDI scheduling at a busy dealer workshop. Customers who have been tracking their vehicle and see it reach "at dealer" status sometimes expect a call within hours; the realistic expectation is two to five business days, and communicating this proactively avoids frustration.
