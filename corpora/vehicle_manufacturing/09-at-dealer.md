# At Dealer — Pre-Delivery Inspection and Dealer Preparation

When a vehicle arrives at the dealer, it enters the final preparation phase before the customer takes ownership. The at-dealer stage covers the period from the moment the transporter delivers the vehicle to the dealer's compound, through the dealer's pre-delivery inspection (PDI), to the point at which the vehicle is ready for customer handover. Depending on the dealer's PDI schedule and workshop capacity, this phase typically takes two to five business days.

## Vehicle Arrival at the Dealer

The delivering transporter (a road car carrier, or, for premium marques, a dedicated delivery driver) presents the vehicle to the dealer's delivery bay along with a transport condition report. A dealer receiving technician — often called a compound controller or PDI co-ordinator — performs a walk-around inspection to check the vehicle's condition against the transport report. Any damage that occurred in transit is documented on the receiving inspection form and photographed. The dealer then notifies the logistics partner and the manufacturer's warranty or claims team as required.

The protective measures applied at the plant and port — seat covers, mirror caps, paint protection film on bonnet and sill edges, any full-body cover — are removed at this point or retained until PDI begins, depending on the dealer's workflow.

## Pre-Delivery Inspection (PDI)

The pre-delivery inspection is a structured check performed by a factory-trained technician to verify that the vehicle is in the correct specification, all factory-installed systems are functioning, and the vehicle is ready for safe operation before it is handed to the customer. PDI procedures are defined by the manufacturer in a PDI checklist — a standardised document that ensures every vehicle receives the same preparation regardless of which dealer performs it.

A typical PDI checklist covers:

**Exterior.** Panel gaps and flushness checked against specification; paint surface inspected for transport-related marks, chips, or contamination; glass inspected for chips or cracks; exterior lights verified operationally; door seals and weatherstripping inspected.

**Interior.** All trim pieces verified for fit and absence of damage; seat adjustment and heating/cooling function verified; instrument cluster illumination verified; all infotainment and navigation inputs tested; all storage compartments and closing mechanisms checked.

**Mechanical and electrical.** Tyre pressures set to delivery specification; brake fluid level checked; coolant level and condition checked (or, for electric vehicles, thermal management coolant verified); all lights tested including headlamp aim; wiper blade condition and washer fluid fill verified; horn and exterior access systems tested.

**Software provisioning.** For modern connected vehicles, PDI includes a software and connectivity check: the vehicle's telematics unit is activated with the market-appropriate SIM configuration if not already done at the port processing centre; navigation map currency is confirmed; over-the-air (OTA) update compatibility is verified; and in many programmes, the vehicle's connectivity account is linked to the customer's pre-registered profile so the connected vehicle app shows the vehicle as "at dealer" with real-time status.

**Battery and charging check (electric vehicles).** For electric vehicles, PDI includes a state-of-charge check — the vehicle is typically delivered to the customer at a defined initial charge level, commonly 80–100%. The dealer verifies that the battery management system reports no active faults and that the charging inlet and onboard charger are functioning correctly.

## Accessory Fitment

If the customer ordered factory-approved accessories — a tow bar, roof rails, rear parking sensors, all-weather floor mats, or a specific in-vehicle experience activation code — these are fitted during the PDI workshop visit. Accessories that are dealer-fitted must be installed by technicians certified for those components to ensure the warranty remains valid. The PDI record documents all accessories fitted, their part numbers, and the technician who installed them.

## Customer Notification

Once PDI is complete and the vehicle has been certified as ready for delivery, the dealer's sales team contacts the customer to confirm a handover appointment. The manufacturer's order tracking system advances the vehicle to "Ready for Delivery" status. For dealers integrated with the manufacturer's connected-vehicle platform, this status update may trigger an automated push notification to the customer's app.

## What the Customer Should Do Before Collection

Before the collection appointment, customers should confirm with the dealer: the insurance policy that will cover the vehicle from the moment they take the keys, any finance or registration documents that need to be brought, and whether they want a comprehensive handover demonstration of the vehicle's features or a briefer induction. Dealers should be prepared to walk through any infotainment, charging, and connected-app pairing steps that the customer has not already completed digitally.
