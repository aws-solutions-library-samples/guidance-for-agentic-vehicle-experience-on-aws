# Ford F-150 — Driver Reference

Excerpts from the Ford F-150 Owner's Manual covering the things drivers
ask about most often: tire pressure, fluids, common warnings, simple
DIY maintenance.

## Tire Pressure (Cold) — Recommended

These are the pressures listed on the door-jamb placard. Always check
when the tires are cold (parked at least 3 hours, or before driving
more than a mile).

| Trim / Tire Size | Front (PSI) | Rear (PSI) |
|---|---|---|
| F-150 (275/65R18 standard) | 35 | 35 |
| F-150 (275/55R20) | 35 | 35 |
| F-150 (265/70R17 base) | 35 | 35 |
| F-150 Lightning (electric) | 38 | 38 |
| F-150 Raptor (315/70R17) | 35 | 35 |
| F-150 with HD payload package | 40 | 50 |
| F-150 spare (compact) | 60 | — |

**Always verify against the actual sticker on your driver's door jamb.**
Aftermarket wheels or load-rated tires may require different pressures.
The placard is authoritative.

### When to Adjust Pressure

- **Heavy load or towing**: add 5 PSI to the rear (do not exceed sidewall max).
- **Cold weather**: pressure drops ~1 PSI per 10°F. Recheck after temperature swings.
- **Long highway driving at high speeds in summer**: do NOT bleed pressure to compensate
  for heat — the placard already accounts for normal heat buildup.

## Inflating a Tire — Step by Step

You can safely inflate a low tire yourself if it's holding pressure.
Here's how:

1. Park on level ground, engine off.
2. Find the recommended pressure on the door-jamb sticker (driver's
   side, between door hinges).
3. Remove the valve cap from the affected tire.
4. Connect the inflator (gas-station compressor, portable inflator, or
   on-board if equipped).
5. Inflate in short bursts. Check pressure with a gauge between bursts.
   Do not over-inflate — release with the gauge's bleed valve if needed.
6. Replace the valve cap.
7. Drive a few miles, then recheck cold pressure the next morning.

### When NOT to DIY (call for service)

- Tire is visibly damaged (sidewall bulge, cut, exposed cord)
- Tire goes flat again within minutes of inflating (puncture)
- TPMS warning persists after inflating to spec (sensor or slow leak)
- You're not sure what the recommended pressure is

## TPMS Warning Light

| Indicator | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Solid amber TPMS | One or more tires below threshold (~25% under spec) | Check all four, inflate to placard spec, drive 10 min for system to relearn |
| Flashing TPMS (60-90s, then solid) | TPMS sensor fault | Schedule service; manual pressure checks until repaired |

## Maintenance Schedule (Normal Service)

| Interval | Service |
|---|---|
| Every 7,500 mi or 6 months | Oil & filter, tire rotation, multi-point inspection |
| Every 30,000 mi | Engine air filter, cabin air filter, brake fluid test |
| Every 45,000 mi | Spark plugs (3.5L EcoBoost), transmission fluid inspection |
| Every 60,000 mi | Spark plugs (5.0L), coolant inspection, brake fluid flush |
| Every 100,000 mi | Spark plugs (3.5L PowerBoost), transmission fluid change |

### Severe Duty (towing > 5,000 lbs, dusty environment, heavy idle)

Cut intervals by ~30%:
- Oil: every 5,000 mi
- Air filter: every 20,000 mi
- Transmission fluid: every 30,000 mi

## Driver-Fixable vs. Service Items (Quick Reference)

**Safe to DIY:**
- Top off washer fluid
- Top off tire pressure (per spec above)
- Replace cabin air filter (behind glove box)
- Replace wiper blades
- Reset oil-life indicator after DIY oil change

**Schedule service:**
- Engine oil pressure warning (red)
- Coolant temperature warning (red)
- Brake system warning (red)
- Airbag/SRS warning
- Persistent check-engine light, especially flashing
- ABS / Traction Control warnings
- Anything you're unsure about

When in doubt, schedule service. Driver safety always wins over saving
a service appointment.
