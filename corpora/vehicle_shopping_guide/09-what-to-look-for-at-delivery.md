# What to Look For at Vehicle Delivery — PDI, VIN Check, and the Handover Walk-Through

Vehicle delivery day is one of the most underutilized opportunities in the car-buying process. Most buyers are excited to drive away as quickly as possible, and dealerships routinely schedule deliveries in a way that discourages thorough inspection. A careful delivery inspection — covering the pre-delivery inspection (PDI) record, the VIN, and a structured exterior and interior walk-through — takes less than 30 minutes and can surface issues that are far easier to resolve before you drive away than after.

## Pre-Delivery Inspection (PDI): What It Is and What to Confirm

A **Pre-Delivery Inspection (PDI)** is a standardized series of checks the dealership performs on every new vehicle before it is presented to the customer. The PDI covers:

- Removing protective wrapping and transport film from exterior panels, glass, and interior surfaces
- Checking fluid levels (engine oil, coolant, brake fluid, windshield washer fluid) and topping up as required
- Checking and inflating tire pressures to the manufacturer's specification
- Confirming all infotainment, navigation, and connected services are functioning and up to date on software
- Verifying that all factory-fitted accessories and options on the order are present and correctly installed
- Conducting a functional test of all exterior lighting, windows, sunroof, and door locks
- Charging a BEV or PHEV battery to an appropriate level for handover (typically 80 percent)

**Ask to see the PDI checklist**. A reputable dealership should be able to show you the completed PDI document — it confirms that the pre-delivery checks were carried out and signed off. If the dealership cannot produce a PDI record, that is worth noting.

## VIN Verification

The **Vehicle Identification Number (VIN)** is a 17-character alphanumeric code that uniquely identifies every vehicle. It encodes the country of manufacture, the manufacturer, the vehicle type, the model year, the production plant, and a sequential serial number. Verifying the VIN at delivery confirms:

- The vehicle you are taking delivery of matches the vehicle you ordered
- The VIN on the vehicle matches the VIN on all documents: the order agreement, the invoice, the registration document, and the warranty card

**Where to find the VIN**:
- Stamped or embossed on the chassis, typically on the firewall (the metal bulkhead between the engine bay and cabin), on the dashboard visible through the lower windshield on the driver's side, or on the driver's door aperture
- Printed on the vehicle's Certificate of Conformity or European Certificate of Conformity (CoC)
- On the invoice and purchase agreement you signed

Cross-check the VIN on the vehicle against the VIN on your order documents and invoice. A mismatch is rare but should be resolved immediately — it indicates either that the wrong vehicle has been prepared for delivery, or a documentation error that needs correcting before the vehicle is registered in your name.

## Exterior Walk-Through

Before signing the acceptance documents, conduct a full walk around the vehicle in a well-lit area. Check:

**Paint and bodywork**:
- Panel by panel, at a low angle — crouch down and look along the surface with a raking light source (daylight is ideal). Look for:
  - Paint swirl marks or buffer marks left by post-PDI polishing — visible as circular scratches under raking light
  - Orange peel (a texture mismatch that looks like the skin of a citrus fruit) — slight orange peel is normal on most production vehicles; severe orange peel is not
  - Drips, runs, or sags in the clear coat
  - Any chips, scratches, or dents on the edges of panels, around door handles, or on the hood leading edge (chips here indicate the vehicle has been driven)

**Panel alignment and gaps**:
- Check that door gaps are even on both sides and that the doors close with a consistent sound. Uneven panel gaps or a door that requires firm effort to close indicate either transit damage or a repair.
- The trunk lid and hood should align symmetrically with the surrounding body panels.

**Glass**:
- Check all glass panels for chips, cracks, or scratches. Even a small chip in the windshield should be noted and documented before you take delivery — if it worsens to a crack after delivery, proving it was pre-existing becomes difficult.

**Wheels and tires**:
- Check alloy wheels for curb damage. New vehicles are sometimes driven by dealership staff and lightly curbed — minor marks are common but should be noted.
- Confirm tire make and model matches what was specified, and that all tires are the same brand, size, and speed rating.

## Interior Walk-Through

Inside the vehicle, check:

**Upholstery and surfaces**:
- Run your hand over all seat surfaces and check for any cuts, holes, or staining
- Check the headliner for any marks or creases
- Check the dashboard and door panel surfaces for any scratches, fingerprints, or sticky residue from protective film that was not fully removed

**All operational functions**:
- Open and close all doors, including the trunk, from both inside and outside
- Operate all windows from the driver's switch pack and from each door independently
- Check the sunroof or panoramic roof opens, tilts, and closes smoothly
- Test all air conditioning vents for airflow
- Confirm all interior lights (reading lights, ambient lighting if fitted) function
- Test the front and rear heated seats and heated steering wheel if fitted
- Check that all USB ports and charging surfaces function with your device

**Infotainment and connected services**:
- Confirm the navigation map version is current
- Pair your phone via Bluetooth and confirm both call audio and media streaming work
- Confirm wireless CarPlay or Android Auto equivalent functions if specified
- Check whether your connected vehicle app is already linked to the vehicle's VIN — this should have been done as part of the PDI

## At the Handover — What to Sign and When

Before you sign the acceptance document:

1. **Document any defects found during the walk-through** — request that the service advisor note them on the delivery handover form with a commitment to rectify under warranty. A verbal acknowledgment is not sufficient.
2. **Confirm the first service date and mileage** — ask when the first scheduled service is due (mileage and/or time interval) and confirm whether it is included in any service plan you have purchased.
3. **Confirm any backorder items** — if any ordered accessories or options were not available for the delivery date (e.g., a specific floor mat set, an accessory mount), confirm the expected delivery date in writing.
4. **Receive all physical items**: the full complement of keys (confirm how many keys are included for your model), the locking wheel nut key, the charging cable set (for BEV/PHEV), the owner's manual, the service record booklet, and the Certificate of Conformity.

Do not sign the acceptance form until any defects found during the walk-through are documented. Once you sign, demonstrating that a defect was pre-existing becomes significantly harder.

## After Delivery: First 100 Miles

In the days after delivery:
- Check that tire pressures remain stable — a slow leak not visible during the PDI may reveal itself within a week
- Note any new warning lights or alerts that appear on the instrument cluster and photograph them for reference
- Confirm that the connected vehicle app correctly reflects your vehicle's status (battery state of charge, odometer, service reminders)
- Register the vehicle on the manufacturer's owner portal if not already done at the dealership — this activates the warranty and any connected service entitlements
