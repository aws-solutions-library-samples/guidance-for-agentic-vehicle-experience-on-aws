# First Service, Warranty, and Connected Features — Your Post-Purchase Primer

Taking delivery of a new vehicle marks the beginning of a multi-year ownership relationship. The decisions you make in the first few weeks — activating your warranty, scheduling the first service, registering for connected services — shape the ownership experience and protect the resale value of your investment. This guide covers the three most important post-purchase tasks for new car owners.

## The First Service

The **first service** is the initial scheduled maintenance visit at the dealership or authorized service center. Its purpose and timing vary by manufacturer and powertrain type:

### ICE and Hybrid Vehicles

For gasoline, diesel, and hybrid vehicles, the first service is typically due at a defined mileage interval (commonly 10,000–15,000 km or 6,000–10,000 miles) or a time interval (12 months, whichever comes first). The first service typically includes:

- Engine oil change and oil filter replacement
- Air filter inspection (replacement if needed)
- Brake fluid condition check
- Tire pressure check and rotation if specified by the manufacturer
- Inspection of all drive belts, hoses, and fluid levels
- Software update check for the engine control unit and infotainment system
- Confirmation that any open technical service bulletins (TSBs) are addressed

**Consult your owner's manual**: the specific items and intervals in the first service are defined in the scheduled maintenance section of the owner's manual. Dealership recommendations to service earlier or more frequently than the manufacturer's published schedule should be queried — they may be appropriate for specific conditions (e.g., very short trips, dusty environments) or they may be upselling routine services ahead of schedule.

### Battery-Electric Vehicles (BEVs)

For a BEV, the first service checklist is shorter because there is no engine oil, oil filter, air filter, or timing belt:

- Tire inspection, pressure check, and rotation
- Brake fluid condition check (brakes are used less on a BEV due to regenerative braking, but brake fluid still absorbs moisture over time)
- Brake caliper inspection (in urban driving with heavy regenerative braking, physical brakes are infrequently applied and calipers should be checked for corrosion-related sticking)
- Software update confirmation for the powertrain management, battery management, and infotainment systems
- 12-volt auxiliary battery check (BEVs have a small conventional battery for powering accessories and the main contactor; this battery can fail independently of the high-voltage pack)
- High-voltage battery health report — many manufacturer service tools can produce a battery state-of-health report showing usable capacity relative to the original specification

**Annual BEV service cost is typically 40–50 percent lower than an equivalent ICE vehicle** because several major service items are absent. Over a five-year ownership cycle, this cost difference is significant.

## Understanding Your Warranty

New vehicle warranties are issued by the manufacturer and cover defects in materials and workmanship for a defined period. Understanding what is and is not covered prevents misunderstandings when a warranty claim is needed.

### Standard Vehicle Warranty

Most new passenger vehicles carry a standard warranty of 3–5 years, with some manufacturers offering 7-year standard warranties. The warranty covers:

- Defects in materials (faulty components as supplied by the manufacturer)
- Defects in workmanship (failures caused by the manufacturing process rather than normal wear or external damage)

The warranty does NOT cover:

- Normal wear and tear items: brake pads, brake rotors, wiper blades, tires, and cabin air filters are maintenance items that wear out in normal use and are excluded from warranty
- Damage from accidents, vandalism, or misuse
- Damage from modifications or the fitting of non-approved accessories
- Failures caused by servicing at non-authorized workshops — in most markets, you can use an independent garage for routine servicing without voiding the warranty, provided the service is performed to the manufacturer's specification and documented. Know your local legislation on this point.

### Battery Warranty for BEV and PHEV

Because the high-voltage battery pack is both the highest-value component in a BEV and the component most affected by use patterns over time, most manufacturers provide a separate, longer battery warranty — typically 8–10 years or 100,000–150,000 miles (160,000–240,000 km), whichever comes first.

The battery warranty typically covers:
- Total battery failure (the pack will no longer power the vehicle)
- Capacity degradation below a defined threshold — typically 70 percent of the original rated capacity as measured in controlled conditions

**The capacity threshold matters**: if your warranty covers degradation below 70 percent, a battery that has degraded to 75 percent is outside the warranty's coverage even if you are unhappy with the range reduction. Understand the specific threshold in your warranty documentation and track your battery health in the manufacturer's connected app.

### Extended Warranty and Service Plans

Extended warranties and prepaid service plans are typically offered at the time of purchase or within the manufacturer's ownership period. Evaluate these based on:

- The specific components covered and excluded
- Whether the plan is transferable to a new owner (improves resale value)
- The total prepaid cost versus an estimate of serviced items at retail price over the plan period
- The cancellation and refund policy if you sell the vehicle before the plan expires

## Activating and Using Connected Features

Modern vehicles incorporate connected features that require activation through the manufacturer's app or an online owner portal. These services enable remote monitoring, over-the-air software updates, trip analytics, and proactive service reminders. Activating them early in your ownership maximizes the value you receive.

### Owner Portal Registration

Register your vehicle on the manufacturer's owner portal within the first week of delivery. Registration typically requires:

- Your full name and contact details
- The vehicle's VIN
- Confirmation of your ownership (typically via the invoice or registration document)

Once registered, the portal provides access to connected service features, digital documentation (owner's manual, warranty documentation, service history), and the ability to book service appointments.

### Remote Vehicle Access

Connected vehicle apps typically provide:

- **Remote locking and unlocking**: useful for checking whether you left the vehicle locked and for letting a trusted person into the vehicle remotely
- **Climate pre-conditioning**: start the cabin heater or air conditioning before entering the vehicle, so it is at a comfortable temperature when you get in (particularly valuable for BEVs, where pre-conditioning from grid power preserves battery range)
- **Battery state of charge and charge status** (BEV/PHEV): monitor your battery level, check whether the vehicle is currently charging, and schedule charging to start at off-peak electricity rate hours
- **Trip history and energy efficiency**: review past journeys with energy consumption data

### Over-the-Air Software Updates

Manufacturers increasingly deliver improvements to vehicle software via over-the-air (OTA) updates — the same mechanism used by smartphones. OTA updates can:

- Improve powertrain calibration (optimize energy use or performance)
- Add new infotainment features or update the navigation map
- Correct software defects identified after production
- Update connected service features

For OTA updates to work, the vehicle needs to be connected to a Wi-Fi network (or have an active data SIM, depending on the manufacturer's implementation). Check how your vehicle receives OTA updates in the owner's portal or manual and confirm that your vehicle's connectivity service subscription is active.

### Service Reminders and Proactive Alerts

Connected services typically include proactive service reminders — push notifications to your phone when the vehicle's onboard monitoring estimates that a service interval is approaching. These reminders are generated from vehicle sensor data (oil quality sensors, mileage tracking, brake wear sensors) rather than fixed calendar intervals, which means they are customized to your actual usage.

To make the most of service reminders:

- Ensure your notification permissions are enabled for the manufacturer's app
- Confirm that the service center linked to your vehicle in the portal is your preferred location
- Review any proactive alerts promptly — an alert about brake pad thickness or tire wear is the system doing its job; ignoring it allows normal wear to approach a safety limit

## Ownership Transfer

If you sell the vehicle during the warranty period, understand what transfers to the new owner:

- The standard vehicle warranty (most manufacturer warranties are transferable to subsequent owners for the remaining duration)
- The battery warranty for BEV/PHEV (confirm transferability — most are, but terms vary)
- Any prepaid service plan (confirm whether it is transferable or refundable)
- The vehicle's connected services subscription — the new owner will need to re-register the VIN in their own owner account, and any personal trip data or preferences linked to your account should be removed before sale
